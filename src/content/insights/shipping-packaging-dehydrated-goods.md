Dehydrated agricultural powders — onion, garlic, ginger, and psyllium husk — are hygroscopic by nature. They absorb ambient moisture readily, which makes packaging design and container preparation critical to preserving quality during international shipping.

## The Moisture Problem

A standard ocean freight container will experience temperature swings of 15–25°C between day and night during a tropical transit route. These swings cause condensation inside the container walls — a phenomenon known as "container rain." Without proper protection, this condensation drips onto cargo and causes:

- **Caking and clumping** of fine powders
- **Microbial proliferation** when moisture content rises above safe thresholds
- **Discolouration** and loss of volatile aroma compounds

## Packaging Layers

We use a multi-barrier packaging system designed to isolate the product from all external moisture sources:

1. **Inner liner**: A food-grade polyethylene (PE) bag, heat-sealed to create an airtight barrier.
2. **Outer bag**: Multi-wall kraft paper (typically 3-ply) that provides structural rigidity, puncture resistance, and printable surface area for labelling.
3. **Palletisation**: Bags are stacked on fumigated wooden pallets and stretch-wrapped with industrial-grade cling film.

## Container Preparation

Before loading, every container undergoes a pre-shipment inspection:

- **Dryness check**: Walls and floor are inspected for residual moisture or rust spots that could indicate seal failure.
- **Desiccant placement**: Calcium chloride desiccant strips are hung from container hooks at regular intervals to absorb ambient humidity throughout the voyage.
- **Temperature loggers**: Wireless data loggers record internal temperature and humidity at 15-minute intervals, providing a complete chain-of-custody record for the buyer.

## Why It Matters

Packaging failures are the single most common cause of cargo claims in the dehydrated food trade. Investing in proper multi-barrier packaging and container preparation is not an optional extra — it is a fundamental requirement for maintaining product integrity and buyer trust across international supply chains.
