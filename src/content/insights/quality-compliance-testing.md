Exporting food ingredients to highly regulated markets like the European Union and the United States demands absolute transparency in quality parameters. Before any consignment leaves our warehouse, it must pass a series of strict laboratory tests.

## Microbiological Standards

Dehydrated ingredients must meet low plate count standards to prevent spoilage in final formulations. Our standard testing protocols cover:
- **Total Plate Count (TPC)**: ≤ 50,000 CFU/g (standard limit).
- **Yeast & Mold**: ≤ 100 CFU/g.
- **Salmonella & E. coli**: Absent in 25 grams.

We perform these tests in ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratories to ensure that every Certificate of Analysis (COA) is legally and scientifically valid.

## Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs)

Pesticide residue is a critical check for import clearance. Different countries have different acceptable limits for chemical residues.
- **US FDA Guidelines**: Focuses on specific pesticide tolerances.
- **EU Regulations**: Usually applies stricter limits (often at the limit of quantification, 0.01 mg/kg, for unauthorized substances).

Through close collaboration with farmers in Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala, and Assam, we manage pest control protocols to ensure compliance with the destination country's regulations.

## Physical Specifications

Beyond chemical compliance, the physical properties of the powders must be consistent:
1. **Mesh Size**: Checked via mechanical sieving to ensure uniform granule distribution.
2. **Moisture Content**: Kept below 6% for onion/garlic and below 10% for ginger/psyllium to prevent caking.
3. **Color & Appearance**: Verified against standard color cards under standardized lighting.
