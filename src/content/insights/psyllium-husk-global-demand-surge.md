Psyllium husk — the outer coating of the Plantago ovata seed — has moved from a niche dietary fibre supplement to a mainstream ingredient demanded across food, pharmaceutical, and nutraceutical supply chains globally. Understanding the forces behind this demand surge is essential for procurement teams planning their sourcing strategies.

## What's Driving the Surge

### 1. Nutraceutical & Supplement Growth

The global dietary supplements market is projected to exceed $300 billion by 2028. Psyllium husk, as a natural, plant-based soluble fibre, fits squarely into the clean-label and functional food categories that are driving this growth. It is increasingly used in:

- Fibre supplement capsules and powders
- Prebiotic formulations targeting gut health
- Weight management products leveraging its satiety effects

### 2. Clean-Label Food Manufacturing

Major food manufacturers are reformulating products to replace synthetic additives with natural alternatives. Psyllium husk's unique gel-forming properties make it an effective:

- **Binding agent** in gluten-free baking (replacing xanthan gum)
- **Fat replacer** in low-calorie processed foods
- **Texture modifier** in plant-based meat alternatives

### 3. Pharmaceutical Applications

Psyllium remains the active ingredient in several blockbuster over-the-counter laxative brands. Regulatory approvals in the EU, US, and Japan for health claims related to cholesterol reduction and glycaemic control have further expanded its pharmaceutical footprint.

## Supply Concentration Risk

Over 85% of global psyllium production originates from the Kutch and Banaskantha districts of Gujarat, India. This extreme geographic concentration creates supply chain risks that buyers must actively manage:

- **Monsoon dependency**: A poor monsoon season can reduce yields by 30–50%, causing sharp price spikes.
- **Limited alternative origins**: While Australia and parts of southern Europe have experimental cultivation, volumes remain negligible compared to Indian production.

## Implications for Buyers

Procurement teams should consider multi-year supply agreements with verified Indian exporters, maintain strategic buffer inventories, and diversify between nutraceutical-grade and food-grade specifications to maintain flexibility as demand patterns evolve.
