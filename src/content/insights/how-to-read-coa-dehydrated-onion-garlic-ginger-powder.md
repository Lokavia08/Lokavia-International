## What is a Certificate of Analysis (COA) and Why It Matters in B2B Spice Sourcing?

In global food manufacturing, seasoning blending, and industrial snack production, ingredient consistency is essential. A **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** is an analytical report issued by a qualified internal quality control laboratory or an independent third-party testing agency (such as Eurofins, SGS, or Intertek) that certifies a specific production batch matches pre-agreed contract specifications.

![Dehydrated Onion, Garlic, and Ginger Powder Laboratory Quality Analysis](/blog/blog-coa-guide.jpg)

A COA is not just a regulatory formality—it is a legal assurance against:
1. **Production line downtime:** High moisture causes caking in pneumatic conveying lines and automatic auger fillers.
2. **Flavor degradation:** Low volatile oil in ginger or deficient allicin in garlic leads to weak flavor profiles in finished food formulations.
3. **Customs rejection or import detention:** Exceeding destination Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) or finding heavy metal contamination can result in border destruction orders from the US FDA, EFSA, or GCC food safety authorities.

## Section 1: Physical Parameters & Granulometry

The physical profile of dehydrated alliums and spices dictates how easily the ingredient disperses in liquid systems, flows through dry blending hoppers, and affects final food appearance.

| Parameter | Standard Method | Dehydrated Onion Powder | Dehydrated Garlic Powder | Dehydrated Ginger Powder | Commercial Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mesh / Particle Size** | US Standard Sieve / ASTM | 95%+ pass through 80 or 100 mesh | 95%+ pass through 80 or 100 mesh | 98%+ pass through 80 to 120 mesh | Dictates dissolution rate, dusting, and mouthfeel in seasoning rubs and batters |
| **Color & Visual Appearance** | Sensory / HunterLab | Off-white to creamy pale yellow | Pale cream to light tan | Light buff to golden yellow | Visual indicator of thermal abuse, scorching, or enzymatic browning during dehydration |
| **Bulk Density** | Tapped / Poured (g/mL) | 0.55 – 0.70 g/mL | 0.60 – 0.75 g/mL | 0.45 – 0.60 g/mL | Crucial for automated packaging fills, volumetric recipe formulation, and container payload calculations |
| **Extraneous Vegetable Matter** | Visual / Floatation | < 0.5% max | < 0.5% max | < 0.5% max | Measures plant skin, rootlets, or outer papery sheath fragments |
| **Foreign Matter / Metal** | Magnetic Separator / X-Ray | Nil (0.0%) | Nil (0.0%) | Nil (0.0%) | Zero-tolerance for glass, metal, stones, or plastic fragments |

## Section 2: Chemical & Physicochemical Benchmarks

Chemical parameters measure the purity, processing control, and functional potency of the spice powder.

![Laboratory extraction and analytical testing for volatile oil and moisture in spice powders](/blog/blog-coa-testing.jpg)

### 1. Moisture Content (% by mass)
- **Standard Limits:** ≤ 6.0% (Onion & Garlic), ≤ 8.0% – 10.0% (Ginger).
- **Test Method:** Karl Fischer titration or vacuum oven drying at 70°C (ASTA Method 2.0 / ISO 939).
- **Why It Matters:** Moisture levels above 6.5% drastically reduce glass transition temperature ($T_g$), causing severe powder caking, mold development, and accelerated loss of volatile aromatic compounds during sea transit.

### 2. Total Ash vs. Acid-Insoluble Ash (AIA)
- **Total Ash (max %):** 5.0% for onion and garlic; 6.0% for ginger. Measures total mineral content left after incineration at 550°C.
- **Acid-Insoluble Ash (max %):** ≤ 0.5% (Onion & Garlic); ≤ 1.0% (Ginger).
- **The QA Secret:** High total ash with high Acid-Insoluble Ash indicates the presence of **sand, silica, or soil contamination** introduced during raw harvesting or improper root washing.

### 3. Active Aroma & Bioactive Markers
- **Garlic Powder — Allicin Potential / Thiosulfinates:** Premium garlic powder should yield ≥ 3,500 – 4,500 µg/g of allicin upon reconstitution. Tested via HPLC.
- **Ginger Powder — Volatile Oil Content:** ≥ 1.5% – 2.5% (v/w) for standard grades; ≥ 2.5% – 4.0% for high-grade [Organic Northeast Ginger](/products/ginger-powder). Tested via Clevenger distillation apparatus (ASTA Method 7.0).
- **Gingerol & Shogaol Content:** Tested by HPLC to verify pungency and active gingerol fractions (≥ 1.0% – 1.5%).

### 4. Sulfur Dioxide ($SO_2$) Residue
- **Specification:** < 10 ppm (considered $SO_2$-free / non-detectable).
- **Why It Matters:** Some processors use sulfites as an artificial whitening and anti-browning preservative. However, the EU, USA, and Japan mandate strict allergen labeling or complete prohibition for non-sulfited spice specifications.

## Section 3: Microbiological Limits & Sterilization Verification

Because agricultural crops grow close to the soil, raw spices naturally harbor native microflora. The COA must prove that the product has undergone effective pathogen reduction (e.g., steam sterilization or irradiation where permitted) without leaving chemical residues.

| Microbiological Assay | Target Limit (Standard Industrial Grade) | Strict Ready-to-Eat (RTE) / Infant Grade | Standard Reference Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total Plate Count (TPC / APC)** | < 100,000 CFU/g | < 10,000 CFU/g | ISO 4833 / FDA BAM Ch. 3 |
| **Yeast and Mold Count** | < 1,000 CFU/g | < 100 CFU/g | ISO 21527 / FDA BAM Ch. 18 |
| **Coliforms** | < 100 CFU/g | < 10 CFU/g | ISO 4832 / AOAC 991.14 |
| **Escherichia coli (E. coli)** | < 10 CFU/g (or Absent / g) | Absent in 10 g | ISO 16649 / FDA BAM Ch. 4 |
| **Salmonella spp.** | **Absent in 25 g** (or 375 g composite) | **Absent in 375 g** | ISO 6579 / AOAC 2013.01 |
| **Staphylococcus aureus** | < 100 CFU/g | Absent in 1 g | ISO 6888 / FDA BAM Ch. 12 |
| **Clostridium perfringens / Bacillus cereus** | < 100 CFU/g | < 10 CFU/g | ISO 7937 / ISO 7932 |

> **Citation Capsule (Microbiological Safety):** Under US FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) and European Regulation (EC) 2073/2005, spice powders used in ready-to-eat seasonings must provide zero-tolerance validation for *Salmonella* across representative composite lots (minimum $n=5 \times 25\text{ g}$ or $1 \times 375\text{ g}$). Steam-sterilized Indian onion and garlic powder offers effective 5-log pathogen reduction while avoiding irradiation labeling restrictions.

## Section 4: Heavy Metals, Pesticides, and Chemical Contaminants

Global food safety authorities enforce strict Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) and chemical contaminant thresholds. A comprehensive export COA or accompanying contaminant annex must address:

### 1. Heavy Metals Testing (ICP-MS)
- **Lead ($Pb$):** ≤ 1.0 mg/kg (ppm)
- **Arsenic ($As$):** ≤ 0.5 mg/kg (ppm)
- **Cadmium ($Cd$):** ≤ 0.5 mg/kg (ppm)
- **Mercury ($Hg$):** ≤ 0.1 mg/kg (ppm)

### 2. Multi-Residue Pesticide Screening (GC-MS/MS & LC-MS/MS)
Export lots shipped to the European Union (EU Regulation 396/2005), USA (EPA 40 CFR Part 180), or UAE (GSO standards) must be tested against a multi-residue screen of 500+ compounds. Key monitored substances include **Ethylene Oxide (ETO) and 2-Chloroethanol** (combined limit < 0.02–0.05 mg/kg for EU compliance), chlorpyrifos, acephate, and triazophos.

### 3. Mycotoxins & Aflatoxins
- **Aflatoxin B1:** ≤ 2.0–5.0 µg/kg (ppb)
- **Total Aflatoxins ($B1 + B2 + G1 + G2$):** ≤ 4.0–10.0 µg/kg (ppb)
- **Ochratoxin A (OTA):** ≤ 15.0 µg/kg (ppb)

## 4 Common Red Flags to Watch for on Supplier COAs

When evaluating ingredient samples or reviewing shipping documents, QA managers should watch for these four critical warning signs:

```
COA RED FLAG CHECKLIST:
├── 1. "Theoretical" or static round numbers (e.g., exact 5.00% moisture across 10 distinct batches)
├── 2. Omission of ISO/AOAC/ASTA test methodology references
├── 3. Missing NABL accreditation or ISO 17025 certificate stamp on third-party lab reports
└── 4. Discrepancy between COA Batch Number and physical master carton shipping marks
```

1. **Static / Repeated Lab Numbers:** If five consecutive container shipments display the exact same decimal values for moisture, ash, and TPC, the supplier may be issuing a generic "specification sheet" disguised as a batch-specific COA.
2. **Missing Recognized Test Methods:** Valid COAs explicitly declare the standardized testing protocols (e.g., *ASTA Method 2.0 for Moisture, ISO 6579 for Salmonella, ICP-MS for Heavy Metals*).
3. **No Batch Traceability:** The batch number on the COA must correspond exactly with the container packing list, Bill of Lading, and outer carton labels.
4. **Vague Microbial Statements:** Avoid COAs that state "Satisfactory" or "Within Limits" instead of quantified values (e.g., `TPC: 24,000 CFU/g`, `Salmonella: Absent / 25g`).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: What is the difference between a Product Specification Sheet and a COA?**
A Product Specification Sheet outlines the agreed target parameters and allowable tolerances for an ingredient before an order is placed. A Certificate of Analysis (COA) is an actual laboratory report measuring the real, empirical values of a specific finished production lot before shipment.

**Q: Does Lokavia provide third-party independent lab COAs?**
Yes. In addition to in-house factory QC batch analysis, Lokavia provides comprehensive third-party lab testing from NABL-accredited international testing laboratories (e.g., Eurofins, SGS, or TUV) covering pesticide MRLs, heavy metals, ETO, and microbiological parameters upon buyer request.

**Q: What is the maximum allowable moisture in dehydrated onion and garlic powder?**
The international export standard is ≤ 6.0% moisture by mass. Moisture levels below 5.5% are ideal for high-humidity destination ports to prevent moisture migration and lump formation.

**Q: How do I verify whether garlic powder has been irradiated or steam-sterilized?**
Steam sterilization is verified through microbiological clean-count reports alongside negative test results for irradiation (Photostimulated Luminescence / PSL and Thermoluminescence / TL testing under EN 1788 or EN 13751 standards).
