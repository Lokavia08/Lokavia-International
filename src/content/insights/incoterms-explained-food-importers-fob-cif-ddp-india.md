## Why Incoterms Matter in Bulk Agri-Commodity Procurement

In international trade, misunderstandings surrounding freight liability, port charges, or container detention can instantly erode margin on a commercial ingredient shipment. Published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC), **Incoterms 2020** provide 11 globally recognized three-letter trade terms defining:

1. **Cost Allocation:** Who pays for inland trucking, port terminal handling charges (THC), ocean freight, and destination import tariffs.
2. **Risk Transfer:** The exact geographical and physical point where damage or cargo loss shifts from seller (exporter) to buyer (importer).
3. **Documentation Responsibility:** Who procures export phytosanitary clearance, certificates of origin, bills of lading, and import entry permits.

Understanding these distinctions ensures seamless logistics from Indian processing belts to your destination warehouse.

## 1. FOB (Free On Board): The Global Industry Benchmark

**Named Port Example:** `FOB Mundra Port, India` or `FOB Nhava Sheva (JNPT), India`

Under **FOB terms**, the seller is responsible for:
- Transporting the cargo from the processing facility in Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, or Assam to the gateway port.
- Export customs clearance, APEDA documentation, mandatory phytosanitary inspection, and port terminal handling charges (THC).
- Safely loading the containerized goods onto the buyer's nominated vessel.

**Where Risk Transfers:** The exact moment the goods pass over the vessel's rail (are securely stowed on board).

```
Factory / Plant (India) ──▶ Inland Trucking ──▶ Port Customs & THC ──▶ [ON BOARD VESSEL] ──▶ Ocean Transit ──▶ Destination Port ──▶ Buyer Warehouse
│◄────────────────────── SELLER (LOKAVIA) RESPONSIBILITY ──────────────►│ │◄──────────────── BUYER RESPONSIBILITY ────────────────►│
                                                                           ▲
                                                                  RISK TRANSFERS HERE
```

### Why Seasoned Food Importers Prefer FOB:
- **Freight Rate Leverage:** Multinational buyers leveraging annual volume contracts with global ocean carriers (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd) often secure lower container slot rates than individual exporters.
- **Free-Time & Detention Control:** Buyers negotiate extended free days at destination ports (e.g., 14–21 demurrage/detention days instead of standard 7 days), preventing costly demurrage fees during customs inspections.
- **Supply Chain Visibility:** Importers track vessels directly through their preferred freight forwarding integration.

## 2. CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight): Turnkey Port Delivery

**Named Port Example:** `CIF Rotterdam, Netherlands` or `CIF Jebel Ali, UAE`

Under **CIF terms**, the exporter (Lokavia) pays for all export costs, ocean freight, and procures marine cargo insurance to the buyer's destination seaport.

**Crucial Note on Risk vs. Cost:** Although the seller pays for ocean freight and insurance, **risk still transfers to the buyer once the container is loaded on board at the Indian port**. If cargo is damaged at sea (e.g., vessel fire, container wash-over), the buyer claims against the marine insurance policy issued by the seller's underwriter.

### Agri-Commodity Insurance Requirements Under CIF:
- **Institute Cargo Clauses (A) vs. (C):** Under Incoterms 2020 rules for CIF, the seller is only obligated to provide baseline coverage (Institute Cargo Clauses C). However, for sensitive dehydrated foods subject to moisture, water damage, or mold, [Lokavia's export shipping protocol](/insights/export-documentation-checklist-dehydrated-vegetables-spices) arranges **Institute Cargo Clauses (A) — "All Risks" coverage** (including Institute Strike & War Clauses) with an insured value of **CIF + 10% (110%)**.

## 3. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): Complete Hands-Off Procurement

**Named Location Example:** `DDP Warehouse Dock, Hamburg, Germany`

Under **DDP**, the seller assumes 100% of the financial costs, transit risks, import tariffs, customs clearance formalities, and final-mile drayage right to the buyer's warehouse doors.

### Why DDP Is Rare for Bulk FCL Commodity Imports:
- **Cost Inefficiencies:** Exporters must build contingency buffers into DDP pricing to cover destination customs broker fees, potential port examination holds, and fluctuating local drayage surcharges.
- **Regulatory Barriers:** Many destination countries (such as US FDA FCE/SID requirements or European customs VAT regulations) require the importer of record to be a locally registered corporate entity.
- **Where DDP Makes Sense:** DDP is ideal for **air shipments of commercial test samples (200 kg – 1,000 kg)** or emergency production balance orders where the buyer does not want to mobilize their customs brokerage infrastructure.

## 4 Hidden Logistics Surcharges Importers Must Account For

When calculating landed ingredient cost per kilogram, food buyers must account for post-landing surcharges beyond the basic freight quote:

1. **Terminal Handling Charges at Destination (DTHC):** Charged by the destination port terminal for offloading containers from vessel to container yard.
2. **Container Demurrage & Detention:** Charges incurred if containers are held inside the port beyond free-time allowance (demurrage) or kept at the buyer's warehouse beyond the return window (detention).
3. **Customs Examination & Storage Fees:** If destination customs (e.g., US CBP / FDA, EU Border Control Post) flags a container for physical random sampling or MRL pesticide screening, exam fees and bonded storage fees apply to the importer.
4. **Phytosanitary & Plant Health Inspection Fees:** Administrative clearance costs charged by destination agricultural authorities upon arrival.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Can Lokavia quote on both FOB and CIF basis?**
Yes. In our standard quotation process, we provide transparent line-item pricing indicating FOB Indian port (Mundra or Nhava Sheva) alongside estimated CIF landed pricing to your named destination discharge port.

**Q: Who pays for the Certificate of Origin and Phytosanitary Certificate under FOB?**
Under standard FOB terms, the exporter (Lokavia) is responsible for obtaining and paying for all official Indian export documents, including the Phytosanitary Certificate, APEDA Certificate of Origin, and batch COA prior to loading.

**Q: What is the difference between CFR and CIF?**
CFR (Cost and Freight) requires the seller to pay for ocean transport to the destination port, but the buyer must purchase their own marine transit insurance. CIF includes both ocean freight and marine insurance arranged by the seller.
