Sourcing and exporting high-volume dehydrated commodities like onion powder, garlic powder, ginger powder, and psyllium husk requires strict controls over the logistics chain. Unlike fresh commodities, dehydrated products are highly sensitive to moisture absorption and ambient temperature fluctuations during maritime transit.

## Sourcing & Sourcing Hubs

Most premium agricultural commodities originate from specific agricultural belts in India:
- **Onion & Psyllium Sourcing**: Sourced from Gujarat's agricultural regions, where the dry atmosphere is ideal for processing.
- **Garlic Sourcing**: Sourced from Madhya Pradesh.
- **Ginger Sourcing**: Cleaned and machine-dried in Kerala and Assam.

## Maintaining dryness in transit

When transporting dehydrated goods across international oceans, the biggest risk is moisture. A rise in relative humidity inside the container can cause:
1. **Caking**: Powder particles fusing into solid blocks.
2. **Microbiological Activity**: Favorable conditions for mold and bacterial growth.
3. **Loss of Aroma**: Volatile oils dissipating under extreme heat.

To counter these risks, we use multi-wall kraft paper bags with heavy-duty food-grade polyethylene (PE) liners. In addition, silica gel packets are placed throughout the container to absorb any ambient moisture.

## Logistics Planning & Transit Time

We plan shipments with conservative transit windows, ensuring that delays at port transshipments do not affect cargo quality. Choosing direct shipping routes, even at slightly higher rates, is our standard practice to reduce risk for international buyers.
